Youth fashion · previously Campus Sutra

CAHOOT

I didn’t just build a fashion feed. I built a community that could sell.

The brief

The category: Youth fashion

The challenge: A noisy market where everyone is fighting for the same scroll.

Campus Sutra needed more than impressions. It needed people to:

Discover→Trust→Engage→Buy→Come back

So I built the strategy around one question:

What if the audience became the marketing?

01

Influencers, but make them accountable.

Instead of chasing follower counts, I built an influencer framework around relevance + intent + trackability.

Creators were matched with seasonal drops and given trackable links to connect:

Creator content→Click→Purchase→ROI

Result: 3X ROI from creator-led acquisition campaigns.

02

Turn customers into the crew.

I conceived and launched a Brand Ambassador Program from scratch.

The idea? Don’t just ask customers to wear the brand. Give them a reason to represent it.

Everyday consumers became brand advocates, creating a more authentic layer of social proof while reducing dependence on paid acquisition. The community became part of the media plan.

03

One brand. Every discovery window.

Instagram wasn't the only place the audience was looking. So I expanded Campus Sutra's organic ecosystem across:

  • Pinterest → fashion discovery
  • YouTube → video + intent
  • Quora → search-led discovery
  • LinkedIn → brand visibility

Different platforms. Different behaviours. One consistent brand universe.

04

I also got in front of the camera.

Strategy is great. But sometimes you have to pick up the camera yourself.

I directed, shot and edited short-form content around:

Fit checksStyling hacksYouth trendsRelatable fashion moments

The goal wasn't polished-for-the-sake-of-polished. It was: “That’s literally me.”

05

What happens after the click?

The work didn't stop at acquisition. I integrated ORM + community management + performance marketing to make sure the experience after the ad matched the promise before it.

Because one bad comment can kill a conversion. And one great customer interaction can create the next customer.

The big idea

Campus Sutra wasn't just selling clothes. We were building a culture around the clothes.

  • Influencers brought discovery.
  • Ambassadors built advocacy.
  • UGC created relatability.
  • Performance converted intent.
  • ORM protected trust.

THE AUDIENCE WASN'T JUST WATCHING THE BRAND.
THEY BECAME PART OF IT.

Inclusive fashion · celebrity collaboration

INSTAFAB PLUS

What if plus-size fashion stopped asking for permission?

The brief

The category: Inclusive fashion

The problem: A market full of sizing hesitation, category stigma and “Will this actually look good on me?” anxiety.

InstaFab Plus didn't need to quietly enter the conversation. It needed to own it.

So we built a strategy around one simple idea:

Representation + Aspiration + Proof

01

Put a face to the movement.

We used celebrity partnerships to take plus-size fashion from niche to mainstream.

The objective wasn't celebrity for celebrity's sake. It was to shift perception: “Plus-size” from a category descriptor to “Fashion.” Modern. Aspirational. Trend-led.

Celebrity gave the category a bigger stage.

02

Then make it real.

Celebrity gets attention. But real people build trust.

So we paired high-visibility celebrity content with relatable, self-shot UGC and Reels showing:

FitFabric drapeReal bodiesEveryday styling

Because when you're buying fashion online, the real question isn't “Does the celebrity look good?” It's “Will this look good on ME?”

03

Turn buzz into business.

Awareness was only the beginning. I connected the top-of-funnel celebrity buzz with:

Performance ads→Retargeting→Community management→Conversion

People who watched. People who engaged. People who showed intent. We brought them back.

04

Trust was part of the strategy.

In an underserved category, reputation isn't a nice-to-have. It's conversion infrastructure.

I integrated ORM + active community management to monitor conversations, address concerns and build an environment where customers felt:

Seen.Represented.Confident enough to buy.
The impact
  • ⭐ Celebrity scale — strategic celebrity partnerships helped push the brand into mainstream visibility and accelerate awareness.
  • 🎥 Creative scale — an agile content pipeline combining Celebrity × UGC × Trends × Reels.
  • 🔄 Full-funnel scale — awareness connected to acquisition through Buzz → Engagement → Retargeting → Conversion.
The takeaway

You can't build an inclusive fashion brand by simply adding bigger sizes. You have to change how people see themselves in the category.

  • Celebrity created aspiration.
  • UGC created proof.
  • Community created trust.
  • Performance created conversion.

AND SUDDENLY, PLUS-SIZE FASHION WASN'T SITTING ON THE SIDELINES.
IT WAS IN THE SPOTLIGHT.
